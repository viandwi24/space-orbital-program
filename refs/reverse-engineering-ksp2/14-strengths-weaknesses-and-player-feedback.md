# 14 — Strengths, weaknesses and player feedback

**Purpose.** This document records what players loved and hated about Kerbal Space Program 1 (KSP1) and Kerbal Space Program 2 (KSP2), traces the KSP2 timeline from announcement to abandonment, and sets out what developers, journalists and players said went wrong. It also surveys competing and successor games. The goal is to give Orbital, a small-team browser KSP-like, evidence for scope and product decisions. All facts come from public sources. Tags: **[KSP1]**, **[KSP2]**, **[KSP2 roadmap — never shipped]**, **[Other: name]**. Statements marked *(unverified)* come from background knowledge that was not re-checked in this pass.

> **Corrections to the brief.** (a) The **launcher** controversy was a **KSP1** event in **November 2022**: Private Division added a launcher, players called it DRM and data collection, and negative reviews followed [S16]. The **May 2024** review bombing of KSP2 (and reportedly KSP1 [S2]) was triggered by the **Intercept Games closure and layoffs**, not by a launcher or EULA change [S1][S28]. No source found here ties a May 2024 EULA change to it. EULA and privacy worries were raised in KSP2 Steam discussions at the February 2023 launch, but that is player discussion, not reporting. (b) Take-Two sold Private Division in **November 2024**, not May 2024 [S11].

## 2. What players loved

### KSP1
- **Failure as fun and emergent stories.** Rockets that explode, stranded kerbals and improvised rescue missions became shareable stories. The game is a comedy of engineering where each failure teaches something. Its critical reception (Metacritic 88, many 9/10 reviews, a Game Developers Choice Audience Award, a Golden Joystick, Edge PC Game of the Year) reflects that loop [S2].
- **Learning real orbital mechanics.** Players come away understanding Hohmann transfers, gravity turns and Δv intuitively. That led to **KerbalEdu** (2014, with TeacherGaming: lessons, metric units, a better UI) [S2] and to public engagement from NASA and ESA. ESA-collaborated BepiColombo and Rosetta parts shipped in 1.10 [S2].
- **Mods and community.** A large mod ecosystem (Kerbal Engineer, MechJeb, kOS, Realism Overhaul, visual mods) and a YouTube tutorial culture kept the game alive for more than a decade. Steam reviews still sit at "Overwhelmingly Positive" (96%) [S3].
- **Staying power.** Real-world spaceflight events still bring players back: Artemis II produced a 2026 resurgence [S2][S18].

### KSP2
- Players praised the **graphics, sound and quality-of-life changes** even while panning the launch [S1]. They also liked the **onboarding ambition** (see 13-ui-ux-patterns.md).
- *For Science!* was praised for bug fixes and Exploration Mode, and recent reviews briefly rose to about 82% positive [S1][S14]. A review a year after launch called the game "much improved" but still unfinished [S25]. This shows how fast a credible content-and-fix update can move sentiment.

## 3. What players hated

### KSP2
- **Price versus state.** USD 50 for an Early Access game with thin content, no modding support and no multiplayer [S5][S26].
- **Performance.** An RTX 3080 recommendation and low frame rates near planets [S5]. Developers promised optimisation throughout Early Access [S5].
- **Physics bugs that undermined the core.** **Orbital decay** of unpowered craft broke the promise that orbits are stable; 0.1.4.1 mostly fixed it, with leftover edge cases [S15]. **Wobbly rockets** were addressed in *For Science!* with extra joints similar to autostrut [S15]. Players split between "fix it" and "keep some wobble, but make it optional" [S15].
- **Fewer players than the original.** Reports noted KSP2 sometimes had fewer concurrent players than KSP1 [S27].
- **Abandonment.** The closure in May 2024 led to the review bomb [S1][S28]. KSP2 is still on sale as Early Access with no developer [S4]. The forum outage in October 2024 added to the sense of abandonment [S17].

### KSP1
- **Launcher and publisher distrust.** The 2022 launcher was criticised as unwanted, mod-breaking and hard to disable [S16].
- **Long-standing UX gaps** (Δv, orbit info, part clutter); see 13-ui-ux-patterns.md.
- **Console ports** were rough until the Enhanced Edition (see doc 13).
- **Old engine limits:** wobbly part joints, large-craft lag and memory limits before 64-bit *(unverified specifics; widely reported)*.

## 4. Why KSP2 development went the way it did

Public reporting gives several overlapping causes. These are drawn from reporting, not insider certainty.

1. **Corporate disruption early on.** Bloomberg (Jason Schreier) reported that Take-Two tried to buy Star Theory, the talks failed over terms, and Take-Two then set up its own studio and recruited Star Theory staff [S6][S7][S8]. Wikipedia says over a third of staff moved [S1]; other accounts say more than half [S6]. Either way, the team, tools and schedule were reset during a pandemic.
2. **Scope.** The pitch covered a new engine-level physics and rendering stack, colonies, interstellar travel, multiplayer, consoles and better onboarding. Creative director Nate Simpson said in 2020 that the team knew it had taken on "an immense technical and creative challenge" [S29]. Only the base sandbox and *For Science!* shipped. Everything else is **[KSP2 roadmap — never shipped]**.
3. **Rebuild versus reuse.** The team chose a new codebase instead of evolving KSP1 *(widely discussed; framing unverified)*. That meant re-solving problems KSP1 had already fixed, such as orbit stability on rails and joint rigidity. The orbital-decay and wobble bugs are exactly regressions of that kind [S15].
4. **Engineering leadership.** Community discussion claims the engineering director left and was not replaced. This is unverified forum hearsay and is recorded only as player perception.
5. **Early Access pricing and expectations.** Charging full price for a pre-content build turned Early Access into a judgement of the finished product [S5][S26].
6. **Communication.** Optimistic pre-release trailers set expectations the build did not meet. Weekly bug-status posts during 2023 were appreciated [S15], but the publisher's silence and denials around the 2024 closure [S1][S9] damaged trust.
7. **Publisher portfolio decisions.** Take-Two cut about 5% of staff (around 600 people) in 2024, closed Intercept and Roll7, and sold the label [S9][S11]. KSP2's fate followed corporate strategy rather than the game's trajectory: it was improving after *For Science!* when it was shut down [S14].

## 5. Player experience (evidence)

- **KSP1 Steam (2026-10-17):** "Overwhelmingly Positive", 96% of 84,735 English reviews; recent 97% [S3].
- **KSP2 Steam (2026-10-17):** "Mostly Negative", 25% of 22,404; recent "Very Negative" (16% of 73); the store warns that the last developer update was over two years ago [S4].
- **Launch week 2023:** about 50% positive of 9,000+ reviews; refund requests [S5].
- **After *For Science!*:** monthly reviews about 82% positive [S14].
- **May 2024:** recent reviews fell to "Overwhelmingly Negative" after the closure news [S1][S28].
- **2026 resurgence:** Wikipedia says KSP1 passed its all-time concurrent peak after Artemis II [S2]. A Ukrainian outlet reported a peak of about 12,000, which it called the highest since 2015 but still below the launch peak [S18]. The figures conflict; check SteamDB before quoting either.

## 6. Competitors and spiritual successors

- **[Other: Juno: New Origins]** (formerly SimpleRockets 2; Jundroo; Early Access 2018, full release Jan 2023). **Procedural, stretchable parts** (tanks, wings, fairings) instead of a fixed catalogue; **Vizzy** visual programming for autopilots and custom MFDs; a career mode with contracts, tech points and a tech tree; Planet Studio; modding [S23]. Steam "Very Positive", 88% [S23]. Players criticise its bland career, steep learning curve and unintuitive UI [S24]. The SimpleRockets line began on mobile *(unverified for Juno's current mobile status; the Steam page lists Windows/macOS only [S23])*.
- **[Other: Kitten Space Agency (KSA)]** (RocketWerkz, Dean Hall). It grew out of RocketWerkz's unsuccessful bid to develop KSP2 [S19]. It runs on the in-house open-source **BRUTAL** C# framework [S22]. It is **moddable by design**: core content loads as a mod [S19], though mod support at first public release was said to be limited [S22]. A free pre-alpha is distributed through Ahwoo (Earth–Moon test system, no loading screens) [S22], funded by community contributions that passed USD 200k by July 2026, when it also won a NZ Hi-Tech award [S21]. A former SpaceX engineer is on the team [S20].
- **[Other: Spaceflight Simulator]** A 2D mobile-first rocket builder with a huge casual audience. It shows that simplified, touch-native spaceflight sells *(unverified figures)*.
- **[Other: Children of a Dead Earth]** A hard-science orbital combat sim with real engineering numbers. It shows a niche audience for unforgiving realism with spreadsheet-style UIs *(unverified)*.
- Space Engineers is a survival/building game, not an orbital-mechanics game, and is out of scope.

## 8. Known problems / criticism (summary)

- KSP2 launched at full price before its core simulation was stable [S5][S15].
- The roadmap promised a sequel-sized feature list; most of it never shipped [S1].
- The publisher's 2020 and 2024 decisions disrupted development twice [S6][S9].
- Communication gaps around the closure, and a forum outage, left players without information [S1][S17].
- KSP1 shows distrust of publisher additions such as the launcher [S16]. Its strengths depended heavily on mods.

## 9. Implications for Orbital (lessons)

1. **Adopt**: make *failure is fun* the core loop. Fast relaunch, readable failure causes ("decoupler fired before engine ignited") and replays. Most of KSP1's appeal comes from this [S2].
2. **Adopt**: physical honesty is non-negotiable. Orbits on rails must never decay, and determinism plus regression tests on orbit propagation guard this. KSP2 lost trust over this exact bug [S15].
3. **Avoid**: a sequel-sized roadmap. Ship a stable sandbox, then add systems one at a time. KSP2's never-shipped colonies, interstellar travel and multiplayer show the cost of promising scope [S1].
4. **Adopt**: free or low-friction access while the game is incomplete. Orbital's browser delivery and KSA's free pre-alpha [S22] avoid KSP2's mismatch between price and state [S5].
5. **Improve**: the public roadmap page should label items as *shipped / in progress / exploring* and never show dates for unstarted work. Honest placeholders apply to promises too.
6. **Adopt**: one big, credible update can turn sentiment around (*For Science!* took recent reviews to about 82% [S14]). Bundle fixes with a new mode instead of only shipping fixes.
7. **Adopt**: mod-first architecture, with stock content as a mod, as KSA does [S19] and as KSP1's community effectively did. Orbital's TypeScript SDK is its version of KSP1's longevity.
8. **Improve**: compared with Juno, offer procedural parts plus scripting (kOS-like) inside a career that is not bland [S23][S24]. Contracts should create stories, not chores.
9. **Avoid**: anything that resembles a launcher, telemetry or account wall. Players punished KSP1's launcher [S16]. Keep Orbital client-side, offline-capable and transparent about data.
10. **Adopt**: performance budgets from day one, on mid-range laptops and tablets. KSP2's RTX 3080 recommendation became the headline [S5].
11. **Improve**: lean into education (KerbalEdu, NASA/ESA collaborations [S2]). Real-mission scenarios and classroom-friendly no-install links fit a browser game.
12. **Adopt**: community continuity independent of any publisher. Keep the forum, wiki and mod index exportable or open so a 2024-style shutdown [S17] cannot erase them.
13. **Avoid**: rewriting what already works. For a small, AI-assisted team, keep evolving the v1 RC instead of restarting. KSP2's new codebase brought back already-solved bugs [S15].
14. **Adopt**: ride real-world events (Artemis and similar) with scenario drops. KSP1's 2026 resurgence shows that demand is event-driven [S2][S18].
15. **Improve**: the scientist, engineer and investor fantasies should each have a visible numbers-first dashboard. That differentiates Orbital from KSP2's science-only progression and Juno's thin career.
