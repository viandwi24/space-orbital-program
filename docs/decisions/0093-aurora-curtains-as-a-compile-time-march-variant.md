# 0093 — Aurora curtains: a few steps through an emission shell, a compile-time variant of the planet pass

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Plan:** plans/061-aurora-curtains.md
**Builds on:** ADR 0027 (the planet pass), 0053 (frame budget), 0064 (half-resolution rung), 0069 (ozone, absolute altitudes), 0070 (shafts as a gated second march).

## Context

The night sky had stars and nothing else. At a high latitude it should show an aurora: curtains low in the sky, a red upper fringe, moving slowly; from orbit a faint ring about the pole on the night side (`docs/product/visual-direction.md`: ready to ship to the public). Budget (ADR 0053 amendment): `medium` at most 13.3 ms and `high` at most 16.7 ms sync on the M5, and the aurora must cost nothing measurable where it cannot be seen, which is almost the whole game (the day side, the low latitudes, the launch site at 0.009 rad of latitude).

## Decision

1. **Emission, not scattering: footprint times height profile, ray marched** (Lawlor and Genetti 2011, research `three.md`). `aurora-model.ts` is the tested pure model and the CPU oracle (`auroraAlongRay`); `shaders/aurora.glsl.ts` is its GLSL twin and takes every constant from the model. The planet pass marches `auroraSteps` (`low` 0, `medium` 8, `high` 12) jittered steps through the shell 80 to 320 km above the surface (the part of the ray inside the shell, one stretch or two when the ray dips into the hollow under it) and sums `profile(height) * footprint(magnetic colatitude, magnetic local angle, time) * night`. Vertical rays and curtain edges come out of the geometry: a ray through the sheet edge-on collects a long path, a vertical one a short one.
2. **The height profile** is three normalised lobes with the colours of the lines: 557.7 nm green (peak 112 km, 7 km below and 22 km above: a sharp lower edge and a tail), the N2+ violet fringe (98 km, narrower, share 0.25) and the 630 nm red (210 km, broad, share 0.14). Sources and what is not sourced (the red height, the oval width) are in research `three.md`; the red share and widths are art choices and say so in the model.
3. **The footprint** is the auroral oval about the body's magnetic pole, a gaussian ring at colatitude 23 degrees at magnetic midnight and 12 degrees at noon (Feldstein), 3.2 and 2 degrees wide, folded along its length by three travelling waves (whole turns per `period` of 600 s, so the animation is a pure function of time and repeats exactly: `auroraTime`), broken into streaks along it and into patches. The sheets are widened by the sample spacing (`filter`, energy preserving) and the streaks fade out when the spacing is wider than their period, so a few steps along a long limb ray average the sheet instead of sampling it at random.
4. **A magnetic pole for every body.** The magnetic axis is the rotation axis (+Y, body-fixed) tilted 9.4 degrees toward longitude -72 degrees (Earth's dipole tilt); magnetic midnight is the meridian away from the sun. One convention for all bodies, nothing per body to author. A mod's body switches the aurora off with `atmosphere: { ..., aurora: false }` (additive field of `bodyDefinitionSchema`, on unless `false`); the planet pass today draws only the first body's atmosphere (draft 071 brings later ones), so it is read for that body.
5. **The march is a compile-time variant, switched by the gate.** The planet pass is one large fragment shader and a long branch that is never taken still lengthens it (registers, instruction cache), so the march is not left in the program that draws the day: `AURORA_STEPS` is a define, the pass uses the program without the march until `auroraStrength` is above 0 and swaps to the program with it (`setAuroraLive`; three keeps both programs once compiled), and `precompile()` builds the aurora variant of every preset that has one in the background (`auroraVariantFor`). This was a precaution: a build with the march always compiled in read about 1 ms slower at the pad and 3 km, but that comparison was against a base built before plan 060's commits and the same-HEAD base read the same as the variant build (journal 061), so the cost of a never-taken branch was **not** isolated; the variant is what was measured and shipped. The gate (`auroraStrength`, pure, tested) is 0 with a bright sky (the star brightness of ADR 0053 stands for the dark sky: night, or thin air above the atmosphere), 0 when the nearest edge of the oval is farther than 0.7 of the central angle at which the camera can still see the shell over the horizon, 0.2 of the strength above the atmosphere (the exposure there is set by the dark ground, not the dark sky: at full strength the orbit view was overexposed), and fades smoothly at the edge of its reach. The shader also weighs every sample by the darkness of the ground under it (`nightWeight`), so the day half of the oval seen from orbit is dark.
6. **Where it is added.** The part of the shell behind the atmosphere is added to the background (`base`) so that the air dims it (a red sky at the horizon) and clouds hide it; the part in front of the atmosphere or the ground, seen from orbit, is added to the final colour. Samples at distances below the atmosphere's entry point are in front, the rest behind.
7. **Half rung and presets.** The march is inside the planet pass, so the half-resolution rung (ADR 0064) draws it at half resolution with no change; `low` has no march (`auroraSteps` 0) and compiles the pass as before.

## Alternatives considered

- **A uniform-gated branch in the one program:** the simplest form; not measured against the variant on equal terms (see decision 5), so the variant stands as the cautious choice.
- **Curtains as geometry (ribbons or a mesh shell):** needs its own draw with depth against the log-depth planet pass, and the sheets from orbit and the ground would be two different objects.
- **A baked footprint texture (equirectangular, like the cloud map of ADR 0070):** not needed: the footprint is a closed form of colatitude, local angle and time, and the folds are time-dependent, which would mean a bake per frame.
- **A dither from the interleaved gradient noise of the rest of the pass:** it drew diagonal stripes on the smooth sheets; per-pixel white-noise jitter reads as film grain, which the sheets can carry.
- **A magnetic axis as a body-definition field:** not added (scope); the convention above is the default and a per-body axis is a later additive field.

## Consequences

- Frame cost: none measurable where the aurora is off (day, low latitude, the journal 039 scene set); about +3.0 ms on `high` and +2.4 ms on `medium` in the aurora scene (journal 061), inside the budget. The first frame in reach of the aurora swaps programs: about 95 ms once per page (the variant is compiled ahead by `precompile()`).
- `PresetSettings.auroraSteps`; `bodyDefinitionSchema.atmosphere.aurora`; `PlanetPass` takes whether the body has an aurora; `Renderer.precompile` compiles the aurora variants.
- From orbit the ring is a soft, wide band; a person should judge whether it wants more structure or a stronger orbit share (`AURORA.orbitShare`).
- Not done: the aurora lighting the ground or the clouds (they do not glow), a magnetic axis per body, sound, aurora on moons (they have no air).
