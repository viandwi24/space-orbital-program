# 0122 — N-body worlds: vessels integrated in the dominant body's frame, bodies on their paths, warp held to a budget

**Status:** accepted (the owner scheduled plan 125 on 2026-10-18; points 3, 6 and 8 amended at claim)
**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Plan:** [125](../plans/125-n-body-gravity-model.md)
**Builds on:** ADR 0095 point 5 (restricted n-body), ADR 0103 point 5 (DE440 for n-body), ADR 0173 (trajectory provider), ADR 0183 (body path models), ADR 0051 (spheres of influence), ADR 0024 (surface rails)

## Context

ADR 0095 reserved a world field `gravityModel: 'n-body'` and decided its shape: bodies stay on rails and vessels feel every body. This ADR fixes what the plan must build: the integrator, the frame, warp, prediction, data and the save. The reference material (`refs/reverse-engineering-ksp2/beyond/05-universe-mode.md`, `09-time-and-real-solar-system.md`) notes that DE440 already contains the real system's n-body solution, that Principia integrates vessels against body ephemerides, and that n-body integration is chaotic: floating-point differences between platforms grow.

## Decision

1. **Bodies are not integrated.** They keep their paths (`kepler`, `kepler-rates`, `series`). Amended at claim: a body names its NAIF id (`naif`, an optional field), and when the host gives the sim the DE440 asset (`createSim({ ephemeris })`) such a body follows the asset within its span (a `table` path inside the sim; outside the span, its own model). No new `orbit.model` value: the definition stays a mod's data, the asset is the host's. A body's path is exact by construction, so the patched and n-body worlds see the same planets.
2. **A vessel is a test particle in the frame of its dominant body.** The sphere of influence stays as the frame choice only (numerical conditioning and display: altitude, orbit read-outs), not as a cut-off of gravity. The acceleration is the dominant body's plus every other body's direct pull minus the frame's own acceleration (the indirect term): `a = −μ₀ r/|r|³ + Σᵢ μᵢ ((rᵢ − r)/|rᵢ − r|³ − rᵢ/|rᵢ|³)`. A frame switch at the old boundary changes coordinates, not the motion.
3. **On rails: an adaptive Bulirsch–Stoer integrator** (modified midpoint, Richardson extrapolation over the step sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, …, step size control from the extrapolation error) with a relative tolerance of 1e-12 and steps bounded by a twentieth of the local orbital period. Amended at claim from Dormand–Prince 8(7): Bulirsch–Stoer needs no transcribed coefficient table, which a mistake in one of its 100-odd rational numbers would quietly spoil, and it is as accurate on smooth gravity. It runs in float64 with a fixed operation order, so a resume on the same platform continues to the bit. The rails state is advanced to the sim's time after every step, so each integration spans at most one step. Across platforms trajectories can drift apart (chaos), which saves make harmless: a save carries the state, not the history.
4. **In flight** (the 2.5 km physics bubble) Rapier keeps its point-mass gravity of the dominant body, and the other bodies' pull is added as one uniform acceleration at the frame's origin; the tidal difference across the bubble (under 1e-9 m/s² at moon distances) is dropped. Surface rails stay as they are: a landed vessel is fixed to its body.
5. **Warp is held to a CPU budget, not to a fixed list.** The integrator's work per real frame must stay under 2 ms on the M5 for all vessels on rails; the sim lowers the reachable warp until it does and says why (`warp` refused or reduced with "n-body integration limits warp to …×"). Measured (amended at claim): the cap is 1e5×, and the budget is counted in acceleration evaluations per real frame (5 000, about 2 ms on the M5 at 0.38 µs each), not in wall time, so the warp a world reaches is the same on every machine and a replay verifies (plan 131). Twenty vessels in low orbit are held at 25 000× (2 861 evaluations a frame); one vessel reaches the cap. The rails take Bulirsch–Stoer steps of at most a twentieth of the local period and read between them by quintic Hermite (centimetres), so a frame does not pay one integration per 10 s rails chunk.
6. **Prediction is numerical.** The n-body trajectory provider (ADR 0173) integrates the same equations forward at tolerance 1e-9 over a horizon of 1.5 periods or 30 days, whichever is shorter, and returns the path as the provider interface's segments: one per frame and sample interval (at most `maxStep`, default 600 s), each starting from the integrated state, so every consumer that draws a segment by propagating its entry conic (the map, rendezvous aids) stays within metres of the integrated path. A new segment end `sample` marks a cut that is not a change of sphere. Maneuver nodes stay impulsive Δv at the node time; their path is the same integration after the burn.
7. **DE440 is a mod asset, downloaded on demand.** The Sol pack (plan 124) ships the Level A paths; a world that asks for DE440 fetches a binary asset (little-endian float64 coefficient records for the Sun, the planets and the Moon over a stated span, gzip), checked by size and SHA-256 and cached in IndexedDB. Measured: 7.5 MB gzipped for 2000 to 2100 (`docs/research/ephemerides.md`), served from `/ephemeris/` and fetched only for an n-body world of the 1:1 Sol system.
8. **No save format bump** (amended at claim): the world's `gravityModel` value `n-body` is accepted, and a vessel's rails state may be `{ kind: 'numeric', r, v, t0, h }` (state, time and the integrator's next step). Both are additive: every older save still reads without a migration, as with the optional fields of plans 095 to 131. The reservation of format 9 is released. Patched worlds and their saves are unchanged.

## Consequences

- Create World's "N-body — Coming soon" becomes selectable when plan 125 ships; patched stays the default.
- Lagrange points, perturbed orbits and gravity assists without SOI cuts become possible; long warps become slower.
- Scripts (`ship.orbit`) read osculating elements in the dominant frame; their meaning changes from exact to instantaneous.
- Bodies integrated as n-body (Universe mode) stay out (ADR 0108).
