# 0043 — Physical terrain: one height function in core, read by the sim and the renderer

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Plan:** plans/031-physical-terrain-and-sky-volumes.md
**Supersedes in part:** [0036](0036-visual-terrain-heights-baked-and-ray-marched.md) points 1 and 3 (heights are visual only; the camera bubble); [0019](0019-ground-tile-and-impact-damage.md) (the tile's top face is the tangent plane of the reference sphere); the "no terrain" notes of [0024](0024-surface-rails-for-controlled-vessels-at-rest.md).

## Context

Plan 027 drew mountains but left the physics ground on the reference sphere, and a camera bubble flattened relief near a low camera so a vessel never sat inside a hill the physics lacked. A lander that touches down on a hill therefore stands in the air or sinks into it on screen, and relief melts as you approach. For a game about landing that is a release blocker. ADR 0036 recorded what physical heights need: a height function both sides can evaluate, a ground tile that follows it, and tests. The function lives in `packages/renderer/src/terrain-model.ts` and reads a biome map from `planet-map.ts`; `sim` may not import the renderer (boundaries), and the renderer may import only `core` and `three`.

## Decision

1. **The height function moves to `@orbital/core`** (`terrain.ts`, with `noise.ts` and `planet-map.ts` that it reads). It is pure float64 TypeScript, environment-neutral and has no dependency besides `kepler` types: `core` is the one package both `sim` and `renderer` may import, so the import table of `boundaries.md` does not change. The renderer keeps what only it needs (ray-march step rule, skyline, GLSL generation) and imports the function, its constants and the octave rotation from `core`.
2. **Shape.** `h(n)` for a unit body-fixed direction `n`: unchanged in structure (land mask from the biome map, hills, ranges, the scenic massif ring, zero within 5 km of the launch site and full strength at 16 km), with two changes made because the height is now physical and sampled at centimetre scale: the ridge `abs` becomes a soft abs (`sqrt(x^2 + eps^2)`), so no crease has unbounded curvature (a bilinear grid of 4 m texels then reproduces it within centimetres, see ADR 0044), and the land mask gets a fractal coast (noise added to the map elevation before the smoothstep), which also ends the stair steps along the coast that the quantised map produced. The map used for heights is the **canonical** one, 1024 x 512, regardless of the graphics preset (the biome map for colour may still be 512 wide on `low`).
3. **`TerrainField`** (`core`): `{ heightAt(direction: Vec3): number; maxHeight: number }`, body-fixed unit direction in, metres above the reference sphere out. `createTerrainField({ radius, latitude, longitude })` builds the canonical map on the first query (about 0.3 s, cached per site) and wraps `terrainHeight`. The launch site is a parameter because the biome map and the flat zone are generated around it. The home body's terrain is **generated world data, not physics**: it is not part of `BodyDefinition`; the host passes the field to both sides (`createSim({ terrain })`, the renderer's `Environment`), and the field carries the same site and radius. Absent, the sim keeps the flat sphere, so every existing test and every headless script is unchanged. `createSim`'s signature and the protocol are source-compatible (an optional option and an optional snapshot field); `API_VERSION` 0.1.8 to 0.1.9 (additive).
4. **The ground tile follows the terrain** (ADR 0019 otherwise stands). Each step, for a flight vessel whose centre of mass is within `GROUND_TILE_ENTER` (300 m) of the terrain (`GROUND_TILE_EXIT` 400 m) the tile is the plane through the terrain point on the radial of the centre of mass, with the terrain normal, found by a central difference of the height function 4 m to each side along the local east and north. The 400 x 400 x 200 m slab is positioned and oriented from that plane, moves with the rotating ground (velocity `omega x p` at the plane point) and `tile.up` is the plane normal. The altitude used for the band, for ballistic debris eligibility (more than 400 m up) and for the "reached the surface" test of on-rails debris is the altitude above the terrain under the vessel; pressure and drag keep the altitude above the reference sphere (sea level), because the atmosphere is a function of the sphere.
5. **Slopes.** There is no slope rule beside the physics: the tile is a rigid plane, friction is 0.8 (ADR 0019), so a vessel **slides** on slopes steeper than `atan(0.8)` = 38.7 degrees and **tips over** when its centre of mass leaves the support polygon of the feet or hull in contact. Terrain slopes stay at or below 45 degrees (`TERRAIN_MAX_SLOPE` = 1). Impact damage keeps ADR 0019's rule, the closing speed along the **tile normal**: a vertical 7.5 m/s landing on a 30 degree slope closes at 6.5 m/s along the normal. A plane fits the terrain under a vessel to centimetres (curvature of the ridges is bounded by the soft abs, the plane is fitted at the centre of mass and follows it every step); a vessel 30 m long on a ridge sees the plane error grow, which is accepted.
6. **Placement.** `placeOnSurface` adds the terrain height at the latitude/longitude and a margin of `slope x footprint radius` so the vessel never starts inside the slope; it settles by falling the last metres. In the flat zone around the launch site the height is exactly zero, so the pad vessel is placed exactly as before.
7. **Rails.** Surface rails (ADR 0024) store the centre of mass in the body-fixed frame, so they hold the terrain pose with no change; the contact pose when the vessel goes on rails is the contact pose when it wakes, so there is no pop. The `landed` flag, the rest test and the debris reaper use the tile's contacts as before. On-rails debris that descends is destroyed when its radius is below the sphere radius plus the terrain height under it (the height is only evaluated below `maxHeight` of the sphere).
8. **Snapshot.** `VesselSnapshot.terrainHeight?: number`: the terrain height (m above the reference sphere) under the vessel's root, present only when the sim has terrain. A host computes radar altitude as altitude minus this.
9. **The camera bubble is deleted.** The renderer draws the physical heights near the camera (ADR 0044) and holds the camera above the terrain instead.

## Alternatives considered

- **Rapier heightfield or triangle-mesh collider.** Both are planar patches, static, and f32: a heightfield of the terrain around the vessel would have to be rebuilt as the vessel moves (a 64 x 64 grid per step per vessel), its creation cost dwarfs the plane update, and the collision against the box compound gains nothing a plane does not give at the footprint of a vessel. The rows/columns constructor `ColliderDesc.heightfield` is documented for fixed environments (`docs/research/terrain-physical.md`). Reconsider for large rovers or bases.
- **A per-step height query and the old sphere clamp.** No friction, no toppling: what ADR 0019 removed.
- **Keep the function in the renderer and re-derive it in the sim.** Two implementations, a parity test against GLSL for each: the bug this plan exists to remove. One function in `core`, one GLSL twin generated from its constants.
- **Put the field on `BodyDefinition`.** The procedural home planet is generated by the renderer's biome map; a mod body has its own generator one day. A field the host supplies keeps `BodyDefinition` data-only and can be replaced per body later without touching the sim.
- **Heights in the mods' data (stock).** Would force the biome map (a generated image) into data or into the SDK; not needed to make landing correct.

## Consequences

- Landing on a hill is physical: the vessel rests on the ground it is drawn on, slides and tips by the rigid-body rules, takes impact damage along the normal, goes on surface rails and wakes without a pop.
- The sim worker now builds the canonical map once (about 0.3 s, off the main thread) and evaluates the height function a handful of times per step per vessel near the ground (4 evaluations, microseconds).
- Altitude readouts keep meaning "above sea level"; a radar altitude is the difference with `terrainHeight`.
- The scenic massif and the hills are now obstacles for ascent trajectories and flat-pad assumptions: the pad's flat zone (5 km) and the sea around the island keep the first kilometres as before. Autopilots that fly low over the massif can hit it.
- The renderer and the sim agree by construction on the function and by test on the grids the renderer actually draws (ADR 0044).
- Follow-ups: radar altitude in the HUD (plan 021 follow-up), terrain-aware trees and buildings beyond the flat zone, rovers and wheels (a tile is not enough for long vehicles).
