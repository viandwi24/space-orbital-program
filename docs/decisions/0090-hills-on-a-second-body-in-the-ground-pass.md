# 0090 — Hills on a body other than the first are drawn by the ground pass (the moon pass with a second ground kind)

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Plan:** plans/059-hills-terrain-on-a-second-body.md
**Builds on:** ADR 0027 (planet pass), 0036/0044 (baked, patched terrain), 0043 (one height function), 0054 (the moon pass), 0064 (planet pass budget), 0067 (terrain kind per body), 0068 (shadows and stones on the moon).
**Research:** none (no external API; the measurements are in [journal 059](../journal/059-hills-terrain-on-a-second-body.md)).

## Context

ADR 0067 let a mod name `terrain: { kind: 'hills' }` on any body and gave the sim the home planet's ridged terrain for it (`createBodyTerrainField`), while the renderer drew a body after the first as a plain sphere (point 4): a lander stood on ground the picture did not show. The planet pass is one fullscreen shader for the first body (atmosphere, ocean, clouds, stars, sun, launch site, shadow-map receiving); the moon pass (ADR 0054) is a mesh pass that draws a body from a baked height cube map and two camera-centred float patches, and the camera is held above the sim's own function.

## Decision

1. **A second ground kind in the moon pass, not a second planet pass.** `MoonPass` takes a ground kind (`craters` with a seed, as before, or `hills`). Everything that makes a body "the ground the sim stands on" is kind-independent and stays: the height cube map of the globe, the two patches, their skirts and cover masks, the bake loop, the patch re-centring, `groundHeight` (the camera clamp, the effects, the vessel's rest test). What changes by kind: the GLSL height function that is baked, the colouring, the bump, and what is switched off. A body after the first whose `terrain.kind` is `hills` gets this pass; `craters` bodies are untouched. `terrain.kind` stays the only choice (ADR 0067); nothing reads `atmosphere` or `parent`.
2. **One function, one text.** The hills bake evaluates the same GLSL `heightAt` as the planet pass's bake: its text moves out of `terrainBakeCommon` into `terrainHeightGLSL` (`shaders/terrain.glsl.ts`, the planet's bake program is the same string as before: a test pins it) and the ground pass appends a `moonHeight(n, radius)` wrapper to it. The biome map (`cachedPlanetMap(TERRAIN_MAP_WIDTH, 0, 0)`, the same canonical map the sim's field reads) is a texture of the pass. The CPU side is `createBodyTerrainField` (the sim's) through `groundHeight`. One difference, found by the first readback: the ground pass reads the map's elevation by hand-made bilinear in float32 (`biomeElevation`, twin of `sampleMap`) where the planet's bake uses the hardware filter, whose 8-bit weights moved a ridge by up to 13 m at a coast on a 250 km body; the readback error then fell from 13.1 m to 0.056 m.
3. **The expensive work follows the camera.** Every ground pass bakes only what the camera is near: patches below 40 km altitude (as now), the cube map when the camera is within `bakeDistance` radii of the body (craters 40 as before, hills 8) and it is released again beyond twice that (hills only; the moon keeps its cube as before). A mod with several hills bodies therefore holds one cube (and patches) at a time unless the camera is among them. Costs per hills body that is baked: a 1024 cube (6 faces, 50 MB RGBA16F, one face per frame), two 512 float patches (4 MB each), the 2 MB biome map. A body whose pass is not baked is one globe mesh drawn with the biome colours and no displacement.
4. **Shading is the pass's own, simple, and honest about what it lacks.** Biome colours from the map (ocean, shore, grass, forest, desert, rock, snow by moisture, temperature, elevation, slope and height), lake floors as water, a grain bump; lit by the scene's sun, hemisphere and shadow-map receiving like the moon. Not drawn on a hills body of the ground pass: the launch site's buildings, vegetation and pad paint; sea waves, glint and foam; clouds; terrain-cast shadows and stones (the march of ADR 0068 assumes slopes of at most 0.5, the hills reach 1: switched off, `uShadowOn` 0 and the code not injected).
5. **Atmosphere is optional and not drawn.** A hills body may have an `atmosphere` in its definition (the sim's drag and the map use it) and the pass draws the same ground with or without it: no scattering, a black sky (the camera on it is lit as an airless body, ADR 0058). An atmosphere on a later body is the planet pass's own work (ADR 0027 consequence) and is draft [071](../plans/071-atmosphere-and-sea-on-a-second-planet.md).
6. **Site and variety.** The ground is the canonical map with its flat grassland at latitude 0, longitude 0, the sim's default surface placement: a second hills planet has the home planet's continents (the map is one seed) at its own radius, so the noise octaves differ but the coasts do not. A `site` or `seed` on the kind is draft 071.

## Alternatives considered

- **A second `PlanetPass` for the later body.** It is the whole fullscreen shader (9 to 12.5 ms at the pad on the M5, ADR 0064), draws stars and the sun as the background a second time, is bound to the environment's sun, shadow receiving, site and cloud map, and every home-planet code path would have to learn which instance it belongs to. A hills body that the camera is not near would still need its globe. Its ray-marched exactness is not needed for a body seen mostly from orbit and landed on at a vessel's scale: the patches are exact to centimetres where a vessel can touch (ADR 0044), the same bound the moon pass has.
- **A fresh third pass.** The moon pass already has the bakes, the patches, the skirt, the cover mask and the camera hooks; copying them is a second place for the same bugs.
- **Parameterising the shaders by `#define` and a second program per kind.** What is done in effect: the material's program is keyed by kind (`moon-hills-patch`), the kinds' code is concatenated at construction, a switch uniform would have run both in every fragment.
- **A smaller cube (512) for hills.** 1.8 km texels on a 600 km body would err by up to 230 m on a ridge at the seam where the 16 km patch ends; 1024 is 920 m, the planet pass's `high` and the figure of ADR 0044. The pass takes the size from the kind, not the preset, so the picture does not change with the governor.

## Consequences

- `scene.ts` creates a ground pass for `hills` bodies after the first (the test of ADR 0067 point 4 is replaced); the body list of the renderer already carries `terrain`. No API change (0.1.23 stays).
- The home planet's bake text is unchanged and the moon's picture is unchanged (the shadows, stones and craters code is not touched, the GLSL bake fragments take their height function by parameter: measured in the journal).
- Not measured on M1/M2, Intel or a phone-class GPU: the 1024 cube bake (6 frames) and the extra biome fetches are by M5 cost.
- A hills body's ground has no shadows cast by hills onto hills and no stones; a vessel's shadow from the sun's shadow map and the Lambert term are all the light there is.
- Measured (journal 059; M5, 1920 x 1080, sync ms, a warm machine, same hour as HEAD): second body `high` 5.2 to 7.5, `medium` 5.9 to 7.4, `low` 4.1 to 5.3 on the surface (flat zone, hillside, massif) and in orbit, against 14.4 and 13.3 of ADR 0053; the cube bake is one 13.5 ms frame, then 8 ms ones for the other five faces. The pad, the moon plain and the crater site of HEAD and this change agree within 0.6 ms over interleaved rounds.
- GPU against float64 (600 texels per patch, three places on each of two bodies, 420 km airless and 250 km with an atmosphere): fine patch worst 0.022 m, mid patch worst 0.134 m.
