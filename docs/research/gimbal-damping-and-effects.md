# Gimbal, fin damping, control surfaces, soft particles, glTF (plan 033)

Checked 2026-10-17. What the models of ADRs 0045-0047 rest on, and what could not be confirmed.

| Fact | Used for | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Thrust vector control: with thrust `T`, gimbal angle `mu` and the distance `l` from the gimbal point to the centre of mass, the pitching moment is `T sin(mu) l`. NASA's MSFC TVC bulletin notes the scalar moment-arm form holds in a 2D one-degree-of-freedom setting; in 3D the cross product `r x F` is the general form. | The engine applies the tilted thrust at its position, so the torque is `r x F` by construction; the mixer picks the deflection from `r x F` | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.05285 (sounding-rocket pitch plane, 2026-10-17); https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240012525 (2026-10-17) |
| Gimbal ranges of the order of a few degrees and actuator response of tens of milliseconds are typical for small TVC systems (a model-rocket gimbal reports +-5 degrees, 44.5 ms mean response). | `gimbal.range` 3 degrees and `rate` 20 degrees per second for the stock LV-T30 (a tuned game value, not a real engine's data) | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00061 (2026-10-17) |
| OpenRocket models a pitch damping moment as a propulsive plus an aerodynamic term; the aerodynamic one sums over components the normal-force slope times the squared distance to the centre of gravity, scaled by speed, density and reference area. The body text of section 3.2.3 of the technical documentation could not be retrieved, so the exact expression is unconfirmed. | The quasi-steady damping of a fin at arm `L`: `-1/2 rho V S C_Na L^2 q` is what `airVelocity` (which includes the rotation) already produces; ADR 0046 adds a stated multiplier on the rotational part | https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~./faculty/flaten/Rocketry_Remote_Lessons_Fall_2021/OpenRocketTechnicalDocumentationVersion13_05.pdf (2026-10-17); https://wiki.openrocket.info/List_of_useful_custom_expressions (2026-10-17) |
| Soft particles: the particle's alpha is multiplied by a saturated, scaled difference of scene depth and particle depth so that billboards do not cut hard lines through geometry. Requires the scene depth as a texture. | Considered and not used (ADR 0047 consequences): the renderer has no depth texture in the transparent pass; the smoke is lit as a sphere impostor instead | https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch23.html (2026-10-17; the depth-fade formula itself came from a secondary slide summary, https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/offscreenparticle/3718755) |
| `GLTFLoader.parse(data, path, onLoad, onError)` takes an ArrayBuffer (a .glb), a JSON string or an object (a .gltf); it does not guess a fallback, the caller chooses; compressed assets need decoders attached (`DRACOLoader`, `KTX2Loader`). | The asset provider: `fetch` in `preload`, `parse` per definition, procedural generators as fallback | https://threejs.org/docs/examples/en/loaders/GLTFLoader.html (2026-10-17) |

Not confirmed online: control-surface effectiveness (a flap of the whole plate is modelled as an all-moving fin: the plate is deflected as a whole about a hinge along its span; real flaps are less effective per degree), and any value of the damping multiplier (a tuned model constant).
